## Tasks Completed

### Task 1: Analyze benchmark discrepancy
**Status:** Complete
**Commit:** c055d12

Created comprehensive analysis comparing two benchmark runs that reached opposite conclusions:
- 2024 Holdout: Single models win 20/21 stats (95.2%)
- 2025 Holdout: Ensembles win 20/21 stats (95.2%)

**Root Cause Identified:**
The benchmarks compared fundamentally different models, not different holdout sets:
- 2024 benchmark: Models trained on 2022-2023 (2 years)
- 2025 benchmark: Models trained on 2020-2024 (5 years)
- Different training windows = different learned patterns

**Key Findings:**
1. More training data ≠ better models (5-year models had worse MAE)
2. COVID-era data (2020-2021) may introduce noise
3. Ensemble "wins" in 2025 were pyrrhic victories (higher absolute errors)
4. 2024 benchmark more reliable - matches production training approach

**Output:** `.planning/phases/19-ensemble-models/BENCHMARK_ANALYSIS.md`

## Outcomes

**Decision Made:** Keep single models with 2022-2025 training data

**Benefits:**
- Simpler architecture (no ensemble overhead)
- Avoids COVID-era noise (2020-2021 excluded)
- Maximizes recency (includes 2025 season)
- More training data than original (4 years vs 2 years)

**Tradeoffs:**
- Does not adopt ensemble approach from 2025 benchmark
- Requires model retraining with expanded window

## Lessons Learned

1. **Training window matters more than assumed** - 2-year vs 5-year windows produced fundamentally different models
2. **Recency vs volume tradeoff** - COVID-era data inclusion degraded performance despite more training examples
3. **Benchmark methodology critical** - Must control for training data differences when comparing approaches
4. **Simple can be better** - Single models with good data beat ensembles with poor data
